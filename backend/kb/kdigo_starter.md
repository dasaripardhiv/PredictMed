# Starter notes (REPLACE)

These are short paraphrased starter notes so the RAG step has something to retrieve. They are not the guideline. Replace this file with your own summaries of the current KDIGO CKD guideline (respect its copyright and licence) and verify every statement with a nephrologist before use.

Chronic kidney disease means abnormalities of kidney structure or function that are present for more than three months and affect health.

GFR categories: G1 is 90 or above, G2 is 60 to 89, G3a is 45 to 59, G3b is 30 to 44, G4 is 15 to 29, and G5 is below 15 mL/min/1.73 m2.

Albuminuria categories by urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio: A1 is below 30 mg/g, A2 is 30 to 300 mg/g, and A3 is above 300 mg/g.

Risk of progression and complications rises as GFR falls and as albuminuria rises, so people in higher-risk categories need more frequent monitoring.

A sustained decline in eGFR of more than 5 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year is commonly described as rapid progression and should prompt a review of causes and of how often kidney function is checked.

Referral to nephrology is generally considered for a GFR below 30, persistent heavy albuminuria, or rapid progression of kidney function decline.

NSAIDs can reduce blood flow to the kidneys and may worsen kidney function, so their use in people with CKD calls for caution and review.

Kidney function tests and albuminuria tests should be repeated on a schedule that matches the person's risk category, and overdue tests are a care gap worth closing.
