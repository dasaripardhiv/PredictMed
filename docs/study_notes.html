PredictMed: my study notes

Team AI Innovix: what it is, how it works, and how to explain it

The big idea in one breath

Kidney disease creeps up silently. About 9 in 10 people who have it don’t know, and health plans usually notice only after an emergency dialysis start. PredictMed watches lab numbers over time, spots a fast downward trend early, explains why in plain words with sources, and gives a care manager a ranked call list. A human always approves the outreach.

Think of it as a smoke alarm for kidneys: it goes off while there is still time to act.

Words I need first

CKD
Chronic kidney disease: kidneys slowly lose their filtering power.
eGFR
Estimated filtration rate, a score for how well kidneys filter blood. Higher is healthier, a falling number is bad news.
UACR
Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Protein leaking into urine is an early damage sign.
KDIGO
The international guideline body. Its stages (G1 to G5) group people by eGFR.
CKD-EPI 2021
The formula that turns a blood test into eGFR. The 2021 version is race-free, which is fairer.
HEDIS
Quality measures health plans report. A “care gap” is a recommended check that hasn’t happened yet.
PHI
Protected health information: anything that identifies a patient. It needs extra care.
FHIR
A standard format for sharing health records between systems.

The trend idea (the heart of it)

One lab value says where you are. A series of values says where you are heading, and how fast. PredictMed measures that speed.

  • OLS slope: the best straight line through the readings. Steeper means faster decline.
  • CUSUM: adds up small drifts and rings an alarm when they stack into a real change. It catches the moment the trend bends.
  • DVI (Deterioration Velocity Index): the team’s speed-of-decline score, built from OLS slope plus CUSUM change-point on eGFR and UACR.

How the whole thing flows

1. Data inLabs, claims and meds as FHIR or CSV. Schema validation checks it. Stored in Parquet with DuckDB.
↓
2. SignalseGFR slope, UACR change, HbA1c trend, days since last lab, NSAID flag (some painkillers can strain kidneys).
↓
3. TrajectoryCKD-EPI 2021 eGFR, then OLS and CUSUM, giving the DVI.
↓
4. Risk scoreLightGBM forecasts 12-month progression. Isotonic calibration makes “30% risk” really mean about 30 in 100. SHAP shows which factors pushed the score. Tiers combine KDIGO stage with decline speed.
↓
5. Explain with sources (RAG)The system searches KDIGO guideline text, then an LLM writes a short reason that cites it.
↓
6. Human reviewCare manager approves outreach drafts in English, Telugu and Hindi. Clinician reviews referral ideas.
↓ then ↺
7. Learn and monitorOutcomes retrain the models through MLflow. PSI drift watch warns when new data stops looking like the training data.

Side feature: a graph layer (NetworkX and node2vec) finds members with similar patterns.

RAG and the two LLMs

RAG means “look it up first, then answer.” Guideline text is turned into number vectors with MiniLM embeddings and stored in FAISS, a fast similarity search. The LLM only writes from what was retrieved, so reasons come with citations instead of guesses.

Groq Llama 3.3 70BVery fast. Handles agent reasoning on de-identified text only.
Local Hugging Face LLMRuns in-house. Used whenever PHI is involved, so patient data never leaves.

A router picks which one to use. Note from the slide: confirm the live Groq model IDs before building.

The agents

The system uses LangGraph, which connects small specialist agents into one workflow:

  • a CUSUM trend agent that watches the slope
  • a SHAP-loop agent that picks the most useful features
  • HEDIS care-gap agents that rank who should be contacted first

Why you can trust it (Responsible AI)

  • SHAP explains every score.
  • Time-based validation: train on the past, test on later data, no peeking at the future.
  • Race-free eGFR (CKD-EPI 2021) and a subgroup bias audit.
  • Human-in-the-loop: nothing goes out without approval, and MLflow keeps an audit trail.

Say it out loud

30-second pitch

“Most kidney disease is found late. PredictMed tracks each member’s eGFR and UACR over time, scores how fast they are declining, explains the risk with cited guidelines, and ranks who to call first. A care manager approves every message, and patient data stays protected by using a local model for anything identifiable.”

Why not just use an eGFR cut-off? A single number misses speed. Two people can share the same eGFR today while one is falling fast.

How is privacy handled? De-identified text goes to Groq, PHI stays with the local model, and humans approve outputs.

Why trust the score? It is calibrated, explained with SHAP, and backed by KDIGO citations.

What data did you use? The plan uses a Synthea synthetic cohort first, then a pilot on de-identified data.

Biggest caveat? Market figures are third-party estimates, and the later roadmap phases are plans, so present them as such.
